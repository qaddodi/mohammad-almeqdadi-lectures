# Acute liver failure/ ACLF in ICU

Lecture plan for pulmonary critical care fellows. Prepared for Mohammad Almeqdadi, MD.

**Status:** First draft deck built from this plan: [ALF and ACLF in the ICU.pptx](ALF%20and%20ACLF%20in%20the%20ICU.pptx) (about 60 minutes, 72 slides counting reveal steps, appendix at the end).

**Style reference:** [LECTURE_STYLE.md](../../LECTURE_STYLE.md). Use its question-first, staged-reveal structure, mechanism-based explanations, clinical traps, longitudinal cases, and practical synthesis.

## 1. Educational purpose

The fellow should leave able to recognize the syndrome, prevent avoidable deterioration during the first ICU shift, and preserve the opportunity for recovery or transplantation.

The organizing question is: **“What changes in my ICU management when this patient has ALF, and what changes when this is ACLF?”**

Prioritize decisions the intensivist owns or shares: airway and ventilation, cerebral protection, perfusion and volume assessment, infection/source control, renal support, procedure planning, and early communication with the transplant team. Keep aetiology and transplant assessment tied to these decisions. Avoid a general overview of every chronic liver disease.

Proposed learning objectives:

1. Distinguish severe acute liver injury, ALF, acute decompensation, and ACLF using the clinical context and a named definition.
2. Initiate parallel stabilization, aetiologic testing, and transplant-center discussion.
3. Explain why ammonia, sodium, ventilation, renal support, and perfusion interact differently in ALF and cirrhosis-associated encephalopathy.
4. Evaluate AKI without prematurely assigning HRS, and select vasoconstrictor/albumin strategies with attention to respiratory risk.
5. Interpret prognosis dynamically, distinguishing referral, listing, organ allocation, and decisions about treatment limitations.

## 3. The two cases and reveal sequence

### Case A: New liver injury, deteriorating brain

**Initial display:** Adult without known cirrhosis, several days of nausea and poor intake, very high aminotransferases, modest bilirubin elevation. Medication history is incomplete.

**Ask:** “Is this ALF? What information is missing?”

**First reveal:** INR 3.2, new disorientation and asterixis, hypoglycemia. History and imaging do not establish pre-existing cirrhosis. This is a proposed ALF phenotype, with the mental-status change evaluated for competing causes.

**Ask:** “Which three things happen in parallel?”

**Reveal:** ICU stabilization and cerebral protection, urgent cause-directed workup/treatment, immediate transplant-center discussion. Obtain acetaminophen level and collateral exposure history and start NAC promptly when acetaminophen toxicity is possible. An undetectable late level does not independently exclude that diagnosis [PMID: 36001996].

**Second reveal:** Worsening to grade III encephalopathy, ammonia 180 μmol/L, oliguria, rising lactate despite initial resuscitation, creatinine still only mildly elevated.

**Ask:** “Do you wait for conventional dialysis indications? What is your airway plan?”

**Later reveal:** Aminotransferases decline while INR, lactate, and encephalopathy worsen. Ask whether this proves recovery. Interpret the full trajectory rather than the enzyme trend alone [PMID: 36001996].

Do not predetermine survival or transplantation. End the case with the decisions and reassessment that determine the next step.

### Case B: Cirrhosis, infection, and extrahepatic failure

**Initial display:** Adult with established cirrhosis and ascites, fever, hypotension, worsening confusion, oliguria, and escalating oxygen requirement.

**Ask:** “Is this simply decompensated cirrhosis? What would establish ACLF?”

**First reveal:** Suspected infection, shock requiring vasopressor support, AKI, and hypoxemic respiratory failure. Have learners identify affected systems and distinguish dysfunction from organ failure using the selected ACLF framework.

**Second reveal:** Hypoxemia worsens after additional fluid/albumin while urine output remains poor.

**Ask:** “More albumin? Terlipressin? Norepinephrine? What must you assess first?”

**Reveal:** Reassess perfusion, fluid responsiveness, congestion, cardiac function, pulmonary pathology, and the dominant AKI mechanism. Septic shock requiring vasopressors is strong evidence for an alternative primary AKI explanation. Do not label every cirrhotic patient with oliguria as HRS-AKI [PMID: 38527522].

**Day 2–3 reveal:** Provide organ-support trends rather than a final outcome. Discuss how trajectory, source control, reversibility, and transplant feasibility change the management conversation. Introduce longer reassessment windows only with explicit clinical context.

## 5. Clinical content to include accurately

### Definitions and scope

- **ALF:** acute hepatic injury with liver-related coagulopathy, conventionally INR ≥1.5, and hepatic encephalopathy, generally within 26 weeks, without established pre-existing cirrhosis. Explain recognized presentations such as Wilson disease and autoimmune hepatitis rather than making “no previous liver disease of any kind” an absolute rule [PMID: 28417882], [PMID: 36001996].
- Severe acute hepatic injury with coagulopathy but no HE warrants urgent attention but is not yet ALF. Hypoglycemia, drugs, sepsis, seizures, and other causes can confound assessment of HE.
- **ACLF:** name the definition used. For the main teaching pathway, use the EASL-CLIF organ-failure framework in acutely decompensated cirrhosis. Acute decompensation alone does not establish ACLF. Distinguish CLIF-C OF assessment, ACLF grade, and the CLIF-C ACLF prognostic score [PMID: 37364789], [PMID: 23474284], [PMID: 24950482].
- In a short comparison, explain why APASL/AARC and NACSELD identify overlapping but different populations. The 2025 Kyoto consensus belongs in the reference set. Do not imply one globally interchangeable diagnostic definition [PMID: 39961976].
- Use kidney failure alone and nonrenal single-organ failure as a discussion of why ACLF grade 1 is more nuanced than “one organ failed.” Obtain the actual grading algorithm from the primary guidance before building a final score table.

### Immediate workup and cause-directed treatment

Run three workstreams concurrently: stabilization, aetiology/precipitant treatment, and transplant assessment.

| Question | Proposed content | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is the severity now? | Serial mental status, haemodynamics, oxygenation, urine output, glucose, INR, metabolic panel, lactate, ammonia, blood gas, phosphate and magnesium | Specify sampling and monitoring frequency from the chosen ICU protocol when authoring slides |
| Could this be acetaminophen? | Level, timing, repeated supratherapeutic exposure, combination products, collateral history, prompt NAC when suspected | Rumack–Matthew nomogram applies to an appropriate single acute ingestion with known timing, not undifferentiated established ALF |
| Which viral causes are actionable? | HAV IgM, HBsAg/anti-HBc IgM and HBV DNA as appropriate, HEV testing guided by availability/context, HSV PCR when clinically indicated | Consider empiric acyclovir when HSV is strongly suspected. Do not await every test before treating a compelling time-sensitive diagnosis |
| Could this be autoimmune or toxic injury? | ANA/ASMA/IgG, drug and supplement reconciliation, selected transjugular biopsy if it changes management | Negative serologies do not exclude acute AIH. Steroid decisions require hepatology and a short response reassessment, alongside transplant evaluation |
| Is this vascular, pregnancy-related, or metabolic? | Doppler imaging, pregnancy testing when applicable, haemolysis/Wilson assessment, targeted exposure history | Pregnancy-related disease requires urgent obstetric involvement. Fulminant Wilson disease needs urgent transplant evaluation |
| What precipitated ACLF? | Infection and diagnostic paracentesis, alcohol-associated hepatitis, bleeding, drug toxicity, viral reactivation, ischaemia, adherence/medication changes | A precipitant can remain unidentified. Continue systematic reassessment |

Sources: [PMID: 36001996], [PMID: 39170946], [PMID: 37364789], [PMID: 40348684]. Exact drug doses and infusion protocols are future authoring tasks requiring pharmacy/local-protocol verification.

### Brain, airway, ventilation, and renal support

Give this section substantial attention for the PCCM audience.

- Grade III–IV HE in ALF generally requires intubation. Prepare to prevent peri-intubation hypoxaemia and hypotension. Choose titratable sedation and account for its effects on the neurologic examination [PMID: 39170946].
- Explain head elevation and neutral alignment, avoidance of fever, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia and excessive fluid accumulation. Hypertonic saline is a conditional SCCM recommendation for ALF patients at risk of intracranial hypertension [PMID: 37052436].
- Sodium targets are context-dependent. The 2022 ALF update describes 145–155 mmol/L in a cerebral-edema prevention strategy. The 2024 practical review describes a lower general preventive range. Present the population and purpose, and choose the expert-center protocol explicitly. Never transfer an induced-hypernatremia target into a chronically hyponatremic cirrhosis patient without a correction-safety plan [PMID: 36001996], [PMID: 39170946].
- Low tidal volume ventilation remains relevant with ARDS. Individualize PEEP to recruitment, oxygenation, venous return, and cerebral concerns. Avoid “PEEP is forbidden.” Avoid routine aggressive hyperventilation. Brief rescue hyperventilation for impending herniation is a different intervention from prophylactic sustained hypocapnia [PMID: 32058387], [PMID: 39170946].
- A normal head CT does not exclude raised ICP. Imaging can evaluate alternative structural pathology. Invasive ICP monitoring is not routine. SCCM suggests against it in advanced HE, with very low certainty. Describe any exceptional center-specific use as such [PMID: 37052436], [PMID: 39170946].
- Explain why ammonia is clinically actionable for cerebral-risk assessment in ALF, while in cirrhosis it does not independently diagnose HE or serve as a stool-treatment target. Use μmol/L consistently. Do not reproduce unit errors from secondary literature.
- The 2023 ACG ALF guideline conditionally suggests early CRRT for hyperammonemia with grade II or higher HE even without conventional renal indications, with very low certainty [PMID: 37377263]. Consider early CRRT with rising/severe ammonia, high-grade HE, AKI, or difficult fluid control in ALF. Ammonia around 150–200 μmol/L is a high-risk context in the literature, not an isolated universal dialysis threshold. Integrate trajectory and neurologic risk. Coordinate CRRT fluids with the sodium plan [PMID: 36001996], [PMID: 28859230].
- Treat overt HE in ACLF with precipitant correction and appropriate enteral therapy, protecting the airway and avoiding excessive catharsis. Evidence for lactulose/rifaximin in cirrhosis must not be presented as established ALF therapy [PMID: 37052436].

### Shock, AKI, and the pulmonary cost of treatment

- Evaluate shock phenotype and use serial perfusion and ultrasound findings, rather than assuming all edema represents adequate perfusion or that all low urine output means fluid responsiveness. Norepinephrine is central to vasodilatory shock management. An initial MAP around 65 mmHg in septic shock is a starting point, with organ-perfusion and cerebral considerations guiding individualization [PMID: 41869847], [PMID: 36001996].
- HRS-AKI assessment uses cirrhosis with ascites, AKI by creatinine and/or urine output, lack of improvement within 24 hours after adequate volume resuscitation when indicated, and no strong alternative explanation as the primary cause. A fixed 48-hour albumin challenge is no longer a prerequisite [PMID: 38527522].
- Structural injury and HRS physiology may coexist. Urinalysis, sediment, drug review, haemodynamics, infection assessment, and renal imaging are useful. Do not use a low urine sodium as proof of HRS.
- Albumin has indication-specific uses. Distinguish SBP, large-volume paracentesis, and HRS treatment from infusions simply to normalize the serum albumin concentration. Monitor volume status and stop/reduce albumin with overload [PMID: 38527522], [PMID: 33657293].
- Terlipressin plus albumin can improve HRS reversal but carries respiratory risk. Norepinephrine is an ICU alternative when terlipressin is unavailable or contraindicated. Do not substitute an ineffective oral strategy while a patient has shock requiring IV support [PMID: 38527522], [PMID: 33657294].
- Discuss oxygenation and volume status before terlipressin. The US label contraindicates use with hypoxia or worsening respiratory symptoms and advises avoiding use in ACLF grade 3. Distinguish contraindication wording from warnings and from the limitations of the original trial. Use the current prescribing information before final authoring. L1 below.
- RRT in ACLF should reflect standard indications, volume/respiratory needs, trajectory, goals, and transplant potential. Avoid “dialysis only if already listed.” A time-limited trial can be appropriate while reversibility and candidacy are evaluated [PMID: 38527522].

### Infection, coagulation, and other daily ICU decisions

- Suspected septic shock requires prompt empiric therapy, cultures without delaying treatment, source identification/control, and reassessment of coverage for prior exposure and local resistance [PMID: 41869847]. Perform diagnostic paracentesis promptly when clinically indicated.
- Do not equate systemic inflammation with proven infection. Separate treatment of suspected infection from routine prophylaxis. The ACG 2023 ALF guideline conditionally recommends against routine prophylactic antimicrobials. Distinguish this from empiric treatment during deterioration, and from indication-specific prophylaxis in ACLF with upper gastrointestinal bleeding described by SCCM [PMID: 37377263], [PMID: 37052436].
- INR is important for synthetic dysfunction/prognosis but is an incomplete measure of bleeding risk. Consider procedure, active bleeding, platelets, fibrinogen, other coagulation abnormalities and, when relevant, viscoelastic testing. Avoid automatic plasma solely to normalize INR. This also adds volume and obscures interpretation of the native INR trend [PMID: 36001996].
- Do not withhold urgent diagnostic paracentesis simply because cirrhosis elevates the INR. Avoid universal platelet or INR correction rules for all procedures. High-risk procedures need their own plan.
- Include glucose monitoring, electrolytes, medication dose adjustment and early nutrition. Avoid routine prolonged protein restriction. Nutrition details are supporting material rather than a major detour [PMID: 32058387].
- Briefly mention tense ascites as a contributor to respiratory/renal compromise and distinguish hepatic hydrothorax from infected pleural disease. A blanket chest-tube instruction for uncomplicated hydrothorax should not be copied from an isolated recommendation without specialist review.

### Prognosis, transplant, and goals of care

- Start transplant-center discussion when ALF is recognized or strongly suspected. Do not wait for King's College criteria or a specified MELD score. KCC is prognostic support, not a prerequisite to referral [PMID: 36001996].
- Explain acetaminophen and non-acetaminophen KCC separately. Keep exact cutoffs, lactate modifications, and the ALFSG prognostic model in an appendix to be extracted from the reviewed source during slide authoring. Do not turn an isolated score into a candidacy decision.
- For ACLF, show organ-support trajectory and serial CLIF-C assessment alongside conventional liver scores. Reassess after initial stabilization and over subsequent days. Do not imply one score accurately predicts an individual's outcome with certainty [PMID: 24950482].
- Mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, or RRT do not automatically exclude transplantation. Selection, reversibility, uncontrolled infection, neurologic injury, comorbidities, and trajectories matter [PMID: 37364789], [PMID: 38309288].
- Distinguish transplant referral, recipient selection, listing urgency and allocation policy. The 2026 AASLD/AST candidate-evaluation guideline is an important current source [PMID: 41405234]. Exact US Status 1A criteria require a current OPTN policy check before slides are made. No allocation criteria have been asserted here.
- Discuss parallel palliative care and clearly documented time-limited support where appropriate. A poor prognostic score should trigger a multidisciplinary conversation, not an automatic withdrawal order.

## 6. Evidence discussions worth the audience's time

Use short study questions, methods, outcome, limitation, and application. Keep the remaining papers in notes/appendix material.

| Clinical question | Study and finding to teach | Limit that must accompany the finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “NAC for every kind of ALF?” | Adult non-acetaminophen RCT: transplant-free survival benefit was concentrated in early HE [PMID: 19524577] | Overall survival was not significantly improved. Do not generalize the early-HE result to advanced coma, children, or ACLF |
| “Is ammonia clearance a reason to start CRRT?” | ALFSG cohort links CRRT with ammonia reduction and better transplant-free outcomes [PMID: 28859230] | Observational association, confounding and treatment selection remain possible |
| “What changed in 2026?” | Pilot randomized study of pre-emptive CRRT in ALF with cerebral edema and concomitant plasma exchange [PMID: 42332991] | Comparable 28-day ITT survival, early mortality signal, substantial protocol violations and per-protocol bias. Obtain full text before quoting a final trial effect table |
| “Can plasma exchange help?” | Open randomized high-volume PLEX trial in ALF supports a survival role [PMID: 26325537] | Open-label design and specific regimen/population. Do not extrapolate to all ACLF or all detoxification devices |
| “Does HRS reversal equal survival?” | CONFIRM showed more verified HRS reversal with terlipressin and important respiratory adverse events [PMID: 33657294] | Renal endpoint improvement does not establish a survival benefit. Patient selection and current label matter |
| “Should we treat the albumin number?” | ATTIRE found no benefit for its infection/kidney dysfunction/death composite from targeted albumin infusions [PMID: 33657293] | Hospitalized decompensated-cirrhosis population, not every ICU phenotype. It does not invalidate albumin for specific accepted indications |

Do not calculate NNT or claim a mortality effect from a surrogate endpoint. If a trial is displayed, reproduce population, endpoint, analysis set, denominators and follow-up accurately.

## 7. Visual and discussion asset plan

These are specifications for later authoring, not generated assets.

1. Two patient columns that persist through the talk, with only the currently relevant information revealed.
2. A laboratory trend that challenges “falling AST/ALT means improvement.” Use explicitly fictional data, with separate scales/units and no fabricated clinical provenance.
3. A compact comparison of ALF versus ACLF covering substrate, diagnostic framework, cerebral-risk approach, renal strategy and transplant pathway.
4. A liver-kidney-brain mechanism diagram connecting ammonia clearance, inflammation, astrocyte swelling, renal support and volume balance.
5. An airway/ventilation decision panel specifying what lung protection must account for when cerebral risk is present.
6. A before/after HRS diagnostic pathway showing the change from a mandatory two-day albumin challenge to rapid individualized assessment.
7. A volume/oxygenation timeline for Case B that shows why renal-directed treatment can worsen respiration.
8. A serial organ-support table for ACLF, with clinical judgments and patient goals alongside the score.
9. A transplant communication checklist listing aetiology, time course, baseline liver disease, mental status, organ support, infection, haemodynamics, laboratory trajectory and barriers requiring evaluation.

Use reviewed primary figures as references for the visual content. Check reuse permissions before reproducing figures. Reconstruct comparison tables with faithful attribution where appropriate.

## 8. Modular depth and possible cuts

**Core:** syndrome recognition, early parallel actions, ALF brain/airway/CRRT, ACLF infection/shock/AKI with respiratory safety, transplant referral and trajectory.

**Add with more time:** detailed KCC exercise, complete CLIF-C grading exercise, one PLEX evidence discussion, a non-acetaminophen AIH/DILI vignette, haemostasis/procedure discussion.

**Appendix:** complete aetiologic testing list, exact pharmacotherapy protocols after verification, full score tables, sodium/CRRT prescription coordination, bleeding pathway, unusual causes and center-specific transplant logistics.

If the session is short, reduce the number of evidence discussions and rare causes. Preserve the two-case contrast and the pulmonary/renal tradeoffs.

## 9. Proposed closing retrieval questions

- Which patient needs an urgent transplant-center call before all testing returns?
- Why might a falling ALT make you more concerned?
- When can CRRT be appropriate before severe azotemia in ALF?
- Why is routine lactulose for ACLF not evidence for ALF treatment?
- Why might albumin and terlipressin be the wrong next action in a hypoxemic, overloaded patient?
- What would make you reconsider yesterday's prognosis or support plan?

Proposed final take-home messages: identify the syndrome, stabilize while investigating, protect the brain in ALF, interpret kidney treatment through its effects on the lungs, and refer/reassess early.

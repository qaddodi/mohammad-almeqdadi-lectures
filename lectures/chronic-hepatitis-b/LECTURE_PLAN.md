### Problems found in his serology key

1. Total anti-HBc is shown as "±" in all four chronic phases. In chronic infection total anti-HBc is positive. The "±" belongs in the isolated anti-HBc row, which is missing.
2. Missing rows the room will ask about: isolated anti-HBc (window period, resolved infection with anti-HBs lost, occult), HBsAg-negative phase / resolved infection, anti-HBc IgM positive in a flare of chronic HBV versus acute.
3. The "Immune natural (remote)" row shows anti-HBe as +. True after seroconversion, but anti-HBe isn't part of the screening panel, so it adds noise.
4. DNA and ALT columns use "High", "Elev." and multiples of ULN. Replace with real thresholds (for example above 20,000 IU/mL, 2,000 to 20,000, below 2,000) and real ALT cutoffs once the guideline values are confirmed (AASLD uses 35 for men and 25 for women).
5. Colors: green for negative and red for positive reads as good and bad. Use his house style (black text, yellow for the revealed answer, red only for abnormal values).

- 6.6 Patient 1 at 34: HBeAg positive, DNA 60 million, ALT 24 on two checks six months apart. AASLD/IDSA 2025 R3 defines immune tolerant as HBeAg positive, DNA above 10^7 and persistently normal ALT, and supports treatment if over 40 or if there is at least grade 2 / stage 2 disease (conditional, very low certainty). The dossier notes the guideline text says "over 40" while its algorithm says "40 or more". At 34 with no fibrosis: monitor. Reveal at 41 (same patient, years later): the age rule now applies. Evidence behind it: Kim Gut 2018 (413 untreated immune-tolerant vs 1,497 treated immune-active; 10-year HCC 12.7% vs 6.1%) with its limits, and the counterweight (Jeon: 0% HCC at 5 years when biomarker-defined and FIB-4 under 1.45). This is the slide that shows why the guidelines moved.
- 6.8 Evidence for treating more: Huang Hepatology 2023 (855 patients, 14 centers, indeterminate phase without advanced fibrosis; 10-year HCC 4% treated vs 15% untreated, adjusted HR 0.30), TORCH-B (160 patients, DNA above 2,000 and ALT 1 to 2 x ULN; histologic progression 26% vs 47%, RR 0.56), and ATTENTION interim (734 patients, TAF vs observation; primary composite events 2 vs 9, HR 0.21, 97.5% CI 0.04 to 1.20, did not cross its stopping boundary). State plainly that none of these randomized treatment against HCC.
- 6.9 Elastography cut-offs for staging: EASL lists F2 above 7 kPa, F3 above 8, F4 above 11; WHO uses APRI above 0.5 or elastography above 7 with no DNA threshold. Do not show a single FibroScan 8 for every indication.

**10. Stopping NAs, cure and what's new**

- 10.1 "Can we stop?" Patient 1 after 5 years undetectable. AASLD/IDSA 2025 R5 suggests continuing until HBsAg loss; stopping only in exceptions: no advanced disease, DNA undetectable at least 2 years, qHBsAg under 100, no HIV or HDV, reliable follow-up. The guideline text says 2 years and its Figure 4 says 3, so say "2 to 3 years" and cite the text. Stop-NUC (166 randomized; HBsAg loss 10.1% vs 0 at 96 weeks; retreated 13.9%), RETRACT-B (1,552 patients; HBsAg loss 3.2% at 12 months and 13.0% at 48 months; end-of-treatment HBsAg under 100 predicted loss), FINITE. After stopping: DNA and ALT every 1 to 3 months for 6 months. Retreat for DNA 10,000 or more, ALT above 5 x ULN, bilirubin above 2.5 or decompensation.
- 10.2 Cure definitions (2022 AASLD/EASL conference): functional cure is HBsAg loss with DNA below quantification 24 weeks off therapy; partial cure is HBsAg under 100 with DNA below the limit 24 weeks off therapy.
- 10.3 Bepirovirsen: per the dossier, B-Well 1 and 2 (Hou, NEJM 2026): functional cure at week 72 in 127/650 (20%) and 106/570 (19%) vs 0 on placebo in noncirrhotic patients on stable NA with HBsAg 100 to 3,000; grade 3 or higher adverse events 16% vs 3%. The dossier also reports FDA approval on 20 Oct 2026 (Hibsago, with NA, qHBsAg 3,000 or below, with a Roche companion diagnostic, boxed warnings for hepatic flares and thrombocytopenia). I can't open fda.gov from here, so I can't confirm this myself. It is the headline of the "what's new" slide, so Mohammad should open the FDA page and the label first.
